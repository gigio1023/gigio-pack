# Standalone Review Questions

Use these questions to review a document from its intended reader's perspective. They are not headings to copy, fields to fill, or a requirement to add a section for every answer.

- What is the document about, and why would this reader use it?
- What is its main finding, explanation, recommendation, or task?
- Which background, terms, and relationships must the reader understand to follow it?
- Can the reader understand the main point without the authoring conversation, private codes, or another document?
- What supports the consequential claims, and where can the reader inspect that support?
- Which statements are observations, source reports, interpretations, plans, or proposals?
- What conditions, contrary results, and unknowns could change the conclusion or action?
- Does the form reduce reading effort while preserving the depth and connections that matter?
- Do figures, tables, and their explanations remain understandable in the delivered medium?
- What should the reader understand or do next, and is any claimed ownership or decision actually established?
- Are the contents and links appropriate for the recipients?
- Did revision, conversion, or publication preserve the important meaning and material?

Apply the questions that fit the document's purpose. Repair a missing answer where readers need it, using the smallest useful structure.
